# ECF v4.25 — Auditable relational discovery

v4.25 adds a complete experimental subsystem that takes unlabeled finite
observations and proposes proximity relations from an explicitly declared
family. It uses no neural network and no human-provided relation labels.

## Procedure

1. Validate observation identity, dimensions and finite values.
2. Split observations reproducibly into discovery and held-out partitions.
3. Estimate normalization parameters on discovery observations only.
4. Generate pairwise distances for each partition.
5. Evaluate a declared threshold family and report support, train/validation
   agreement, example pairs and caveats.
6. Rank hypotheses by empirical stability and support.

## Interpretation

A stable result means that a particular proximity rule had sufficient positive
pair support and similar positive-pair rates in this finite split. It does not
mean that the relation is universal, useful, causal, mathematically necessary,
or discovered without inductive bias. The metric family and thresholds are
specified by humans. Euclidean geometry is therefore a baseline substrate,
not the final claim of self-created mathematics.

## Why this is a meaningful gate

Before a cell can develop its own relation language, it needs a testable cycle
for proposing relations, keeping track of evidence and exposing failure. This
module provides that limited cycle for one relation family. A subsequent step
must allow the cell to propose new relation operators and test them against
held-out observations, counterexamples and resource budgets. Network benefits
must be evaluated against isolated-cell and shuffled-experience controls.
