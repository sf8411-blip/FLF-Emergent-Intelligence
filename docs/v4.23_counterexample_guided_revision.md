# ECF v4.23 — Counterexample-guided hypothesis revision

This increment composes the bounded expression evaluator (v4.19), empirical
transform checks (v4.20), and seeded counterexample search (v4.22).

## Workflow

1. Assess a candidate on a seeded discovery sample.
2. Assess it again with a distinct seed as a finite validation search.
3. If a witness is found, pass the candidate and witnesses to an explicit
   revision callback.
4. Reassess the revised candidate, subject to a hard revision budget.
5. Preserve every assessment in the returned history.

The callback is intentionally caller-supplied. A generic workflow cannot infer
how every failed mathematical hypothesis should be repaired. The current module
does not automatically promote a candidate into a persistent language or claim
that a revision is scientifically correct.

## Status meanings

- refuted_discovery: at least one witness was found in discovery.
- refuted_validation: discovery found none, but validation found a witness.
- survived_finite_search: neither finite search found a witness.
- revision_stopped: callback returned None.
- revision_no_change: callback returned the current expression unchanged.
- revision_budget_exhausted: all permitted attempts were used.

No status means mathematical proof. Independent seeds reduce direct sample
reuse but do not guarantee statistical independence in a formal sense, nor
do they cover the whole domain. Bounds, transformations, tolerance and sample
count remain part of the claim. Stronger assurance requires analytical proof,
systematic enumeration where feasible, or additional independently designed
tests.

## Example

For reflection T(x)=-x, the hypothesis f(x)=x is refuted by a witness.
A revision callback may propose f(x)=x^2, which can survive the sampled
search. This illustrates the loop mechanics, not autonomous discovery of a
general mathematical theory.
